Walter Gropius Biography

Introduction

Walter Adolph Georg Gropius was a majorly influential German-American architect that believed in functionality of design over anything else. He pioneered modern architecture as well as founded the Bauhaus school, influencing from glass makers to painters though Bauhaus.

His Life

Walter Gropius was born in Germany on May 18th, 1883. He was fascinated by industrial architecture and it's simplicity, which was even pronounced in his earlier works. His design was mainly focused on simplicity, unity, and efficiency. Later after World War 1, he founded the Bauhaus in Germany, 1919, a school that would produce multiple influential artists in the era. The Nazis shut down the Bauhaus in 1933, which prompted many members, including Gropius himself to escape to other countries, for him, the US. He would establish The Architects' Collaborative, which would have some notable commissions, but never reached the status of his works in Germany.
